\section*{Statistical Notes: Why Minimum-of-Averages is Biased and Quantile Methods Are Not}

\subsection*{Old Method: Minimum of Long-Term Averages}

\begin{itemize}
\item Each bin had a long-term averaged power estimate (low variance due to smoothing).
\item The noise power per bin followed a Gamma distribution (not exponential anymore).
\item The minimum of these bins was selected as the noise estimate.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Why This is Biased}

\begin{itemize}
\item The minimum of random variables is always biased low.
\item The more bins you have, the lower the expected minimum.
\item Smoothing reduces variance but does not remove bias.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Result:}
\begin{itemize}
\item Stable but biased.
\item Slow to respond to real noise changes.
\item Statistically unpredictable bias.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{New Method: Quantile + Correction Factor}

\begin{itemize}
\item Use q-th quantile of bin powers, or average of bins below threshold.
\item Correct the estimate using known formulas from the exponential CDF.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Why This is Unbiased}

\begin{itemize}
\item Exponential distribution has exact relationship between quantile and mean:

\[
\mu = \frac{p_q}{-\ln(1 - q)}
\]

\item Averaging multiple low-end bins is correctable using a mathematically defined correction factor.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Result:}
\begin{itemize}
\item Statistically unbiased (after correction).
\item Low variance when averaging multiple bins.
\item Fast response and mathematically sound.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Summary Table}

\begin{tabular}{|l|l|l|l|l|}
\hline
Method & Bias & Variance & Speed & Mathematical Foundation \\
\hline
Min of smoothed bins & Biased low & Low, unpredictable & Very slow & Poor \\
Quantile (single bin) & Unbiased (corrected) & Higher & Fast & Exact \\
Quantile + average of low bins & Unbiased (corrected) & Low & Fast & Exact \\
\hline
\end{tabular}

\subsection*{Final Conclusion}

\begin{itemize}
\item Minimum of long-term averages looks smooth but hides systematic bias and reacts slowly.
\item Quantile-based estimators, properly corrected, are unbiased, tunable, fast, and mathematically sound.
\item Ideal for SDR, AGC, and real-time adaptive systems.
\end{itemize}
